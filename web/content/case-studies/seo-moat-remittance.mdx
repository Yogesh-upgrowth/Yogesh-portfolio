## Step 4: What Didn't Work (Important)

Not every move paid off. Being honest about failure is as important as celebrating success.

### Writing Long-Form Content Without Intent

High word count does not equal ranking. We wasted weeks on pages that had no real search intent behind them. Word count is a byproduct of thorough coverage — not a ranking factor by itself.

### Copying Competitor Content Structure

When we mirrored competitor page layouts without understanding the underlying intent, we got neither differentiation nor conversion. It looked similar, performed worse.

### Ignoring Conversion Metrics Initially

Traffic increased. Comparison clicks didn't. We were optimizing for the wrong metric early on — tracking sessions but not the North Star action (provider click-through). This cost us a month of misaligned effort.

## Key Takeaways

### SEO is not about content. It's about capturing decisions.

01

Every page on your site should be optimized not just to rank, but to help a user make a decision. If it doesn't serve a decision, it shouldn't exist.

### Competitors don't win because of better writing. They win because of better systems.

02

Monito and CompareRemit didn't outwrite us. They out-systemized us. The solution wasn't better content — it was better architecture.

### Programmatic SEO is the only scalable moat in comparison products.

03

If you're building a comparison or marketplace product, your SEO advantage must be structural — not editorial. You cannot manually write your way to coverage at scale.

### UX is SEO.

04

If your page doesn't help users decide instantly, you lose — to bounce rate, to competitors, to the attention economy. Page speed, comparison widgets above the fold, and instant answers are SEO levers.

### Internal linking is your hidden growth lever.

05

Most teams treat internal links as an afterthought. We treated them as a primary distribution system for authority, crawlability, and user flow.
