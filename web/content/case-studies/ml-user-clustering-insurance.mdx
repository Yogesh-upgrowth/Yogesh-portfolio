## The Insurance Industry's Comfortable Lie

Insurance companies have historically sold one product to "the Indian customer" — a monolithic persona built from aggregated surveys and actuarial tables. Young, salaried, urban, safety-conscious. Send them the same email, the same features, the same pricing tiers.

When we ran our first clustering analysis on 180,000 insurance platform users, we found not one customer but seven distinct behavioral archetypes — each with different purchase triggers, different price sensitivities, and different preferred features. The industry's comfort with the "average customer" had been leaving significant money on the table.

> **What this showed.** The most surprising finding: our highest-converting segment wasn't the young urban salaried buyer the industry optimizes for. It was a 35–50 age segment we hadn't built a single feature for. They were converting despite the product, not because of it.

## The Conversion Rate Anomaly

We'd noticed an anomaly in our conversion data: a small group of users was converting at 4× the platform average, but their demographics looked ordinary. Same age range, similar cities, similar income proxies. What was different wasn't who they were — it was how they behaved.

## One Product, Seven Different Purchase paths

The insight wasn't just that these segments existed — it was that the product had been designed around one of them (the Researcher, roughly) and three others (Price Hunter, Life-Event Buyer, Lapsed Renewer) were converting significantly while being actively underserved.

> We'd built one checkout flow. We'd written one set of email sequences. We'd designed one pricing page. And seven different types of buyers were trying to navigate all of it — some succeeding despite us, most giving up.

The highest-use play wasn't building new features. It was routing each archetype to an experience designed for their specific purchase trigger — using existing infrastructure, different sequencing.

## Archetype Routing System

We built a real-time classifier that assigns users to archetypes within their first 3 sessions, then routes them to differentiated experiences. The classifier doesn't ask users to self-identify — it infers from behavior.

### Behavioral Classifier

Random forest model: session depth, feature usage sequence, time on comparison pages, price filter behavior, brand filter usage. Classifies into one of 7 archetypes at session 3 or after 8+ pageviews — whichever comes first.

### Experience Routing

Each archetype gets different: landing page emphasis, email sequence, comparison UI defaults, pricing display order, and support trigger timing. Same underlying product; different path emphasis based on purchase trigger.

### Lapsed Renewer Fast-Track

Highest-value quick win: Renewal flow reduced from 7 to 3 steps. Pre-filled from previous policy. Single-click renewal for users whose policy expired within 30 days. Conversion rate went from 44.8% to 68.2%.

### Life-Event Detection

Triggered by UTM (wedding searches, baby searches, car searches in preceding days via platform partner signals). Life-Event Buyers shown curated bundles rather than full comparison. Decision simplified.

## Technical Implementation

### Clustering Pipeline

- K-means run on 22-feature behavioral vectors (session-level)

- Cluster stability validated with silhouette scores over 3 months of data

- Archetype labels assigned by business interpretability + statistical separation criteria

### Real-Time Classifier

- Random forest classifier trained on labeled archetype data (ground truth: eventual cluster assignment)

- Classification triggered at session 3 or 8+ pageviews, stored in user profile

- Confidence threshold: > 70% probability to assign. Below threshold: default to Researcher path

### Experience Layer

- Feature flags control which UI variant each archetype sees

- Email system: archetype tag drives sequence selection

- Pricing page: default sort changes by archetype (Price Hunter: price ascending; Loyalty Seeker: brand prominence)

## What Surprised Us

### The highest-converting segment was the most underserved

The Lapsed Renewer converted at 44.8% despite a 7-step renewal flow — the worst UX on the platform. They were succeeding through sheer intent. Optimizing for their specific need moved the needle faster than any other single change.

### K-means found segments business intuition missed entirely

We'd never explicitly thought about a 'Life-Event Buyer' archetype. Business intuition categorized these users as 'first-time buyers.' The clustering revealed their distinct behavior pattern, and the tailored experience showed their purchase trigger was entirely different.

### Confidence thresholds matter as much as model accuracy

A mis-classified user shown the wrong path had lower conversion than an unclassified user shown the default path. Setting a confidence threshold (we used 70%) for routing decisions improved outcomes — it's better to not classify than to classify wrongly.

## Where We Stumbled

### What did not work: Initial clusters were statistically clean but business-meaningless

**Why it broke.** First k=5 clustering produced clean statistical separation but the segments had no interpretable purchase trigger differences. 'Cluster 3' wasn't actionable.

**The fix.** Added business interpretability as a first-class criteria alongside silhouette score. Ran qualitative interviews with users from each candidate cluster to validate behavioral interpretation before acting.

### What did not work: Feature Maximizer path over-complicated the comparison UI

**Why it broke.** Assumed Feature Maximizers would convert better with more filter options. Added 12 new filter dimensions. Conversion dropped — too much choice, decision paralysis.

**The fix.** Narrowed to 5 most-purchased add-on combinations for that archetype. Showed 'most popular for similar buyers' as default. CVR recovered and exceeded baseline.

## Where This Goes

### Dynamic Archetype Migration

Users can move between archetypes over time — a Price Hunter who becomes a Loyalty Seeker. Tracking archetype migration and re-routing in real-time rather than static assignment.

### Predictive Archetype at Acquisition

Use pre-acquisition signals (search query, referral source, landing page) to predict archetype before first session. Start the path correctly from the first pageview.

### Cross-Product Archetype Consistency

Extend archetypes across product categories. A Price Hunter in insurance is likely a Price Hunter in health or motor. Cross-category archetype consistency for unified personalization.

> There is no such thing as "the Indian insurance customer." There are at least seven — each with a different purchase trigger, a different path, and a different definition of value. Build for all of them.
