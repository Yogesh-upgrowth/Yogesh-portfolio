## We Changed Three Words. Revenue Doubled.

That's the headline. It's technically accurate. It's also almost entirely misleading — because we didn't just change three words. We ran 14 failed experiments first, misread the data twice, and almost cancelled the whole program before the winning variant appeared.

This is the version with the messy middle included.

> **What this showed.** The actual insight from this project wasn't about button copy. It was about the psychological state users were in at the moment of CTA — and how wrong our assumptions about that state had been for years.

## The Comparison Page Problem

Our insurance comparison page had a single CTA: "Get Quote." Click-through rate: 6.8%. For a page where users had already spent an average of 7.4 minutes comparing plans — deeply engaged, clearly intent-driven — 6.8% felt inexplicably low.

73% of users left the page immediately after seeing the CTA. Not before seeing it — after. The CTA wasn't invisible. It was actively causing exits. Something about the ask itself was wrong.

## The Assumption We Got Wrong for Three Years

We had assumed users at the comparison stage were ready to buy. That's why the CTA said "Get Quote" — the next logical step in a purchase funnel. But qualitative research (5 user interviews, a quick survey, and session recording analysis) told a different story.

What We Assumed

- → Users at comparison stage = ready to buy

- → "Get Quote" = next logical step

- → Friction is minimal — they've already decided

What Users Said

- → "I'm still not sure — I want to understand more"

- → "Getting a quote feels like committing"

- → "I want to compare one more option first"

> **What this showed.** The comparison stage wasn't decision-ready. It was still uncertainty. "Get Quote" felt like a commitment. Users who weren't certain yet — which was most of them — chose the exit over the uncertainty of what came next.

## Matching CTA Language to Psychological State

We reframed the CTA design problem: instead of optimizing the button copy in isolation, we needed to match the ask to where users actually were psychologically — not where we wished they were.

> The best CTA is the one that feels like the natural next step — not the company's preferred next step. Users at the comparison stage need to feel like they can learn more, not that they're committing.

We mapped 5 psychological states users could be in on the comparison page, and designed CTA variants for each. Then we tested which state was dominant — and which CTA performed best across the broadest range of users.

## Beyond the Button

CTA copy was the visible result. But we also changed the supporting context around the button — the sub-text, the placement, and the anxiety-reducing elements nearby. These compounded the effect.

### Sub-CTA Reassurance Text

- Added below button: 'No payment required. Takes 2 minutes.'

- Removed: 'Best price guaranteed' (too salesy, undermined trust in testing)

- Added: 'You can compare more before deciding' — explicitly gave users permission to continue

### CTA Placement

- Moved from bottom of plan card to inline — visible without scrolling

- Added secondary floating CTA on scroll depth > 60% of page

- Mobile: sticky bottom bar with CTA appeared after 2 minutes on page

### Trust Signals Adjacent to CTA

- Star rating (4.7★, 12,400 reviews) placed within 40px of CTA

- IRDAI logo and SEBI badge directly adjacent — regulatory credibility

- 'X people bought this plan today' social proof — urgency without pressure

## The Testing Timeline

### Weeks 1–3 · Qualitative Research — Understanding Why Users Left

- 5 user interviews: unmoderated session recordings reviewed

- Exit survey added: 'What stopped you from continuing?' (300 responses)

- Key insight: 67% cited uncertainty about what came next

**Result.** Hypothesis: CTA felt like commitment; users wanted to feel low-risk.

### Weeks 4–8 · Batch 1 Testing — First 8 Variants

- 8 CTA variants tested via A/B framework (2-week minimum per variant)

- 4 failed to beat control; 3 beat by \<25%; 1 beat by 68%

- 'Check Eligibility' emerged as early winner

**Result.** Progress, but inconclusive. Continued testing.

### Weeks 9–13 · Batch 2 + Context — Context Layer Added

- 6 more CTA variants + 3 supporting context variants

- 'Start My Cover' emerged: +119% vs control

- Sub-text, placement, and trust signal variants layered in

**Result.** Full package (CTA + context): +2.1× conversion vs original

## What This Actually Taught Me

### 01. The CTA is a promise, not a command

'Get Quote' is a command. 'Start My Cover' is a promise — that the next step is low-commitment, self-directed, and explorable. Users follow promises. They resist commands when they're uncertain.

### 02. Qualitative context is what makes quantitative results interpretable

'See Full Details' at +35% could have been the winner if we'd stopped early. Understanding why users left — the commitment fear — told us there was a bigger open up. The qual insight directed the next 6 experiments.

### 03. Supporting context doubles the impact of copy changes

CTA copy alone: +119% CTR. CTA + sub-text + trust signals + placement: +2.1× full conversion. The button is the peak — but the surrounding context determines whether users climb to reach it.

### 04. 'My' is one of the most powerful words in a CTA

Ownership language consistently outperformed in our testing. 'My Options,' 'My Cover,' 'My Quote' outperformed equivalent variants without 'My' by 30–40%. Users want to feel in control of their next step.

## What Didn't Work

### What did not work: 'Buy Now' made things significantly worse

**Why it broke.** Dropped CTR 40% vs control. Users who were uncertain about commitment responded to urgency language by leaving faster. The opposite of the intended effect.

**The fix.** Removed urgency framing entirely from subsequent tests. Shifted focus to autonomy and low-commitment language. Every subsequent variant outperformed urgency framing.

### What did not work: We almost stopped at 'Check Eligibility'

**Why it broke.** After Batch 1, 'Check Eligibility' was the winner at +68%. We nearly called it done. An additional round of ideation almost didn't happen.

**The fix.** Kept testing. 'Start My Cover' outperformed by another 51% on top. The difference between stopping at +68% and continuing to +119% was one more batch of experiments.

## What We're Testing Next

### Archetype-Specific CTAs

Different archetypes have different decision triggers. Price Hunters may respond differently to Researchers. Testing archetype-specific CTA language for each of the 7 buyer segments.

### Dynamic Copy by Dwell Time

CTA copy changes based on how long the user has been on the page. \<2 minutes: exploratory copy. 5+ minutes: decision-stage copy. CTA adapts to where the user is in their own timeline.

### Micro-Conversion Funnels

Test micro-CTAs before the main CTA. 'Save this comparison' → 'See full policy document' → 'Start My Cover.' Graduated commitment ladder before asking for the main action.

> We didn't double revenue by changing button copy. We doubled it by finally understanding what users needed to feel before they'd take the next step — and then saying exactly that.
