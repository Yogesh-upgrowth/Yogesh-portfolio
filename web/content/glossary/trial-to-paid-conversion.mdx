What is trial-to-paid conversion? It is the share of free-trial starters who
become paying subscribers. The median sits at 42.5% for trials of 17 to 32 days and 25.5% for
trials of four days or fewer. In India and South-East Asia the median is 15.2%.
</AnswerBox>

## How is trial-to-paid conversion calculated?

Divide the trial starters who converted by all trial starters in the same cohort,
then fix the window before you read the number. A 7-day trial measured at day 7
counts nobody who converts late; measured at day 45 it counts renewals as
conversions. Pick one window per product and hold it.

<Example illustrative>
100 trial starters, 30 of whom pay within 14 days of trial end, gives 30%.
</Example>

Two denominators circulate and they are not the same. Trial-to-paid uses trial
starters. Download-to-paid uses installs, so it folds in every step before the
trial and lands far lower.

## What is a good trial-to-paid conversion rate?

Category sets the floor more than execution does. Travel leads at 43.5% and
Photo & Video trails at 22.2%. Compare against your own category, because the
gap between the two is wider than most teams will move their own number in a year.

Trial length moves it more than almost any other lever. Trials of 17 to 32 days
convert at 42.5% against 25.5% for four days or fewer. The long trial is not
being generous. It is buying enough sessions for the product to prove itself.

## Why is the number lower in India?

The regional median for India and South-East Asia is 15.2%, against 34.2% in
North America. Price explains part of it: Indian subscription prices sit at 0.6
times the US baseline, so the same conversion rate earns less and the same price
asks more of a wallet.

Payment mechanics explain the rest. UPI Autopay carried 175 million mandate
transactions in January 2025 against 58 million a year earlier, and is reported
at 92% or better mandate success under ₹15,000 (about $156). The mechanics are
in [UPI Autopay](/glossary/upi-autopay).

<FromMyWork exp="exp-012">
Not supporting UPI was the single most expensive omission in the funnel. Card
and net banking were the only options in a market where the buying demographic
pays by UPI. Integration took six weeks, and 73% of purchases came through UPI
in the first month after launch.
</FromMyWork> If a product still routes Indian
trials through card recurring, some of its lost conversion is a failed debit
rather than a rejected offer.

## Which paywall type converts better?

Hard paywalls reach a 10.7% median day-35 download-to-paid conversion against
2.1% for freemium. That is a download-to-paid figure, not trial-to-paid, so it
is not a like-for-like swap. It does say that gating earlier raises the measured
rate while shrinking the top of the funnel.

Adding a trial to an annual plan raises one-year lifetime value by 35% against a
direct purchase, so the choice is rarely trial against no trial. It is which plan
the trial sits on.

## How is it misused?

The two patterns worth naming. First, quoting a category median as a target: a
gaming app holding itself to Travel's 43.5% is chasing a number its category does
not produce. Second, moving the measurement window after a bad quarter, which
manufactures improvement without changing anything a user experiences.

Price localisation wins 62.3% of experiments, the highest win rate of any test
type, so a low rate in India is usually a pricing test waiting to be run rather
than a reason to lower the target.
