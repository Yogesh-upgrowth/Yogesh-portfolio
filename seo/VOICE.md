# Voice standard — the Lenny bar

02 §9 defines the voice. This is the external benchmark it is measured against,
because "sounds like Yogesh" is easier to judge with a comparison than in the
abstract. Lenny's Newsletter is the bar: the most-read product publication on
the internet, and the one Tier B readers already compare everything to.

## What Lenny's actually does (researched 2026-09-30)

| Element | The standard |
|---|---|
| Opening | A hook of one or two sentences carrying a blunt insight, a surprising result, or a pain the reader recognises. Never a definition, never context first. |
| Register | Casual and tactical. Described as 1:1 writing from a smart operator to peers — not a vendor to a prospect, not a teacher to a student. |
| Structure | Hook → context → tactical breakdown in 3–5 bullets or short paragraphs. What was done, what worked, **and what didn't**. |
| Specificity | Numbers, screenshots, templates, frameworks, message tests, small wins. Density of value, not density of words. |
| Effort | 10–30 hours per major post, written and edited by one person. Starts as bullets and deepens over weeks. |

## What this changes for our pages

Almost nothing in 02 §9 contradicts it — which is the point of checking. Three
things it sharpens:

1. **"What didn't work" is not optional garnish.** Lenny's posts carry the
   failures inline, not in a closing section. Our case studies already require a
   "what didn't work" block; teardowns and playbooks should carry the same
   honesty inside the body. The experience library has the raw material: 400
   pages rewritten after cutting editorial review, a feature killed at 2.8%
   adoption, a value line discontinued after selling 23% of its order.
2. **Peer register, not authority register.** "I'd price this at ₹149" reads as
   a peer. "It is recommended that organisations consider" reads as a consultant
   protecting himself. The banned-phrase list already catches most of the second
   voice; the test is whether a smart operator would send the page to a
   colleague.
3. **Density is the measure, not length.** A 3,000-word playbook that says three
   things fails against a 1,200-word one that says nine. G07's word bands are a
   floor and ceiling, never a target — B13 says this and it is worth repeating
   because padding to a range is the easiest way to fail this bar.

## The one thing we have that Lenny's does not

India. Lenny's audience is global-first and its numbers are US-weighted. Every
page here can carry the India layer — UPI Autopay mandate behaviour, the 0.6×
price index, e-mandate failure rates, GST on digital subscriptions, Play billing
in India — and that is the differentiation that cannot be copied by writing
harder. Where there is nothing India-specific to say, say nothing (01 §C7).
